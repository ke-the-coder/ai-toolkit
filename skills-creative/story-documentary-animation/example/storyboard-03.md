# 分镜 · 神农尝百草（纪录片版）

The third storyboard, as posted at the checkpoint and approved with "approve", taking the recommendation on all four
questions at the end. It adds three kinds of question the first two don't: **how to show a death gently** (two of them,
one the hero's), **a safety reminder the source's parent note asks for**, and **how to show record-keeping before
writing existed**. The finished page was `03-神农尝百草-动画.html`; this folder has it without the recordings,
[03-神农尝百草-无录音.html](03-神农尝百草-无录音.html) (safe to Read and grep).

**Decisions the user approved** (reuse them as precedent):
- **Character look:** the text says only 「有一个人」 who walks, chews, sits and carries a basket, so Shennong is a man in
  human form (short beard, topknot, leaf cape over a hemp tunic, straw sandals, bamboo basket), not the traditional
  「人身牛首」 ox head or horns. The folk "crystal belly" became a documentary body-outline diagram beside him, because the
  text has him *feel* the changes, not see them.
- **Death, gently:** 「再也没有醒过来」 is a far wide shot at dusk with no faces: someone under a blanket of leaves, two people
  kneeling, one leaf falling. 「神农去世了」: he sits against a big tree at sunset and closes his eyes, calm; the light
  turns gold and the leaves from his basket drift out over the land, carrying on into the epilogue. No pain, nothing
  literal about 断肠. That chapter card has no quote (the ending is later folklore).
- **Poisoning, gently:** cartoon spirals for 头晕; for 呕吐 he bends over beside a boulder and nothing is shown; lying still,
  his eyes stay open and blink.
- **A spoken safety reminder** (a new kind of addition): the story's `s` parent note says 不认识的东西一律不许往嘴里放, so
  cue 40, 小提醒 · A LITTLE REMINDER, is spoken and shown after the last story line. check_text.py takes it as `--tail 1`.
- **Record-keeping before writing:** he scratches little pictures (not characters) on bark while a record card fills in;
  real characters first appear on the bamboo book written by later people.

**Questions for you**

1. **What should Shennong look like?** Tradition gives him an ox head or small horns: 《帝王世纪》 and 《补史记·三皇本纪》 both say 「人身牛首」. The text only says 「有一个人」, and he walks, chews, sits and carries 「一个筐」.
   **My recommendation:** a man in human form, with no horns. He'd have a short beard, hair in a topknot tied with a cord and a sun-browned face. He'd wear a cape of leaves over a knee-length hemp tunic, with straw sandals and a bamboo basket on his back. This follows story 02's precedent: base the look on what the text has the character do.
   There's also a folk detail that he had a transparent "crystal belly" (水晶肚子), so he could see what each plant did inside him. The text doesn't have it: he *feels* the changes. So instead of a see-through body, I'd put a documentary body-outline diagram beside him that glows warm for 发热 and cool for 发凉 (cues 18–22, listed as an added picture).
   *Other options:* two small horn-shaped hair buns as a nod to tradition; or the ox head (I don't recommend it for a seven-year-old, and the text says 一个人).

2. **Gentle pictures for the hard moments.** None of these shows pain.
   - 「有人吃了以后就再也没有醒过来」 (7): a far wide shot at dusk. Someone lies still under a blanket of leaves, two people kneel beside them, and a single leaf falls. There's no close-up of a face.
   - The poisonings (24–27): cartoon spirals and a swaying camera for 头晕. For 呕吐 he turns his back behind a rock and bends over, and nothing is shown. When he's lying on the ground unable to move, his eyes are open and blinking while clouds slide by. The 70 counter plays as a wide time-lapse where he's small.
   - 「这一次的毒太厉害了」 and 「神农去世了」 (33–34): his hand slows at the basket, and he sits down against a big tree at sunset. He closes his eyes with a calm face, the light turns gold, and the leaves from his basket drift out over the land. Those leaves carry into the epilogue, landing in people's hands.
